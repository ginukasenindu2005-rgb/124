Niyagama Divisional Secretariat · Southern Province

Welcome to
Pitigala.

Pitigala is a peaceful village located in the Galle District of the Southern Province in Sri Lanka. It belongs to the Niyagama Divisional Secretariat and is home to a warm, close-knit community. The village is blessed with rich natural beauty, featuring green tea gardens, rubber estates, and rolling hills. Pitigala truly shows the beautiful harmony between traditional rural life, community culture, and nature.

Culture & Demographics

Rituals, festivals & the people of Pitigala

The village calendar turns around temple ceremonies, seasonal rites and the harvest of tea — each one binding neighbours together across generations.

Annual · Temple

Katina Ceremony

A cherished robe-offering festival held at the village temple, drawing devotees from across Pitigala to weave, offer and celebrate together within a single day.

Ritual · Folk

Dewol Madu

A traditional ritual performance rooted in folk belief, combining drumming, fire and dance to bless the village and its harvest.

Monthly · Poya

Poya Day Events

On each full-moon Poya, the village gathers for observances, alms-giving and lantern-lit temple visits that mark the rhythm of village life.

Places & Landmarks

Natural wonders & village landmarks

From forest streams to the shared institutions that keep the village running — the map of everyday Pitigala life.

Kalu Dola

A dark-watered forest stream winding through the village's rubber and tea land, popular for quiet walks and freshwater bathing spots.

Pitigala, natural stream

Kaludiya Pokuna

A tranquil, tree-shaded natural pond named for its dark still water — a favourite spot for visitors seeking cool shade and calm scenery.

Pitigala, natural pond
Main Landmark
Featured Building

Athukorala Tea Factory Tea Centre

The Athukorala Tea Factory Tea Centre is Pitigala's major tourist attraction — a working tea factory where visitors can join estate workers for hands-on tea plucking, then follow the leaf indoors to see how it is withered, rolled, fermented and fired into the tea found in cups around the world. It stands as the architectural and economic heart of the village's tea identity.
